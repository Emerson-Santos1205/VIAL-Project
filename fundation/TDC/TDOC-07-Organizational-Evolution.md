# 1. Purpose

This document defines how Cognitive Organizations evolve over time.

Organizational Evolution is the controlled transformation of Organizational Cognitive State through experience, evidence, decisions and learning.

---

# 2. Evolution Principle

A Cognitive Organization evolves through successive state transitions:

```text
S₀ → S₁ → S₂ → ... → Sₙ
```

Each valid transition SHALL remain traceable.

---

# 3. Evolution Cycle

The fundamental organizational evolution cycle is:

```text
Observe
   ↓
Interpret
   ↓
Evaluate
   ↓
Decide
   ↓
Validate
   ↓
Transition
   ↓
Learn
```

The cycle MAY repeat indefinitely.

---

# 4. Observation

The organization receives information from its environment.

Observation alone does not modify organizational cognition.

Information becomes organizationally relevant when incorporated into:

* Context;
* Evidence;
* Knowledge.

---

# 5. Interpretation

Evidence is interpreted against:

* Goals;
* Knowledge;
* Context;
* Policies;
* historical experience.

Interpretation MAY produce new organizational understanding.

---

# 6. Evaluation

The organization evaluates whether the observed condition requires a response.

Evaluation SHOULD consider:

* Goal impact;
* Policy constraints;
* Evidence quality;
* available Capabilities;
* Cognitive Cost;
* risk.

---

# 7. Decision

The organization determines whether a state transition is required.

Not every observation SHALL produce a Decision.

A Decision SHALL be governed and attributable.

---

# 8. Validation

Before producing a significant state transition, the Decision SHOULD be evaluated against:

```text
Goal
+
Evidence
+
Policy
+
Capability
+
Context
```

Invalid Decisions SHALL NOT produce valid organizational evolution.

---

# 9. Transition

A validated Decision produces:

```text
S(t) → S(t+1)
```

The transition SHALL preserve all applicable invariants.

---

# 10. Learning

Experience MAY become organizational Knowledge.

The conceptual learning process is:

```text
Experience
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Validation
   ↓
Knowledge
   ↓
Memory
```

Knowledge MAY subsequently influence future Decisions.

---

# 11. Adaptation

A Cognitive Organization MAY adapt:

* Strategies;
* Roles;
* Capabilities;
* Policies;
* Knowledge;
* operational behavior.

Adaptation SHALL remain consistent with organizational Goals and governance.

---

# 12. Evolution History

Organizational evolution SHALL form a traceable chain:

```text
S₀
 ↓
D₁
 ↓
S₁
 ↓
D₂
 ↓
S₂
 ↓
...
```

This history represents organizational evolution.

---

# 13. Organizational Continuity

Organizational evolution SHALL preserve organizational identity unless an explicit governance process defines:

* termination;
* merger;
* replacement;
* reorganization.

---

# 14. Evolution Quality

Organizational evolution SHOULD improve one or more of:

* Goal achievement;
* Decision Quality;
* Cognitive Efficiency;
* Knowledge Quality;
* resilience;
* consistency;
* auditability.

---

# 15. Failed Evolution

A failed Decision SHALL NOT silently modify authoritative organizational state.

Failure SHOULD produce an auditable record where required.

---

# 16. Evolution and Cognitive Cost

Organizations SHOULD avoid unnecessary evolution.

A state transition SHOULD produce sufficient organizational value to justify its Cognitive Cost.

---

# 17. Evolution and Learning

A successful organization SHOULD accumulate validated experience.

However, learning SHALL NOT mean that every historical observation automatically becomes Knowledge.

Validation remains mandatory.

---

# 18. Evolution Model

The complete model is:

```text
Environment
     ↓
Observation
     ↓
Evidence
     ↓
Interpretation
     ↓
Decision
     ↓
Validation
     ↓
State Transition
     ↓
New OCS
     ↓
Learning
     ↓
Organizational Memory
```
