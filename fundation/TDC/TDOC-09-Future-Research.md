# 1. Purpose

This document defines research directions arising from the Theory of Distributed Organizational Cognition.

It identifies areas requiring further theoretical, experimental and engineering investigation.

---

# 2. Formal Proof

Future research SHOULD investigate formal proofs for:

* state consistency;
* policy compliance;
* invariant preservation;
* decision validity;
* organizational continuity.

---

# 3. Organizational Intelligence

The TDOC requires future quantitative definitions for:

* Organizational Intelligence;
* Decision Quality;
* Organizational Coherence;
* Adaptation Efficiency;
* Cognitive Efficiency.

These concepts SHALL eventually become measurable.

---

# 4. Cognitive Cost

Research SHOULD establish quantitative models for:

```text
Reasoning
Communication
Synchronization
Validation
Memory
Coordination
```

A unified Cognitive Cost model is necessary for large-scale optimization.

---

# 5. Organizational Compression

A central VIAL research question is:

> How much organizational cognition can be represented through compact authoritative state without reducing decision quality?

The research objective is:

```text
Minimum Cognitive Representation
+
Maximum Organizational Utility
```

---

# 6. Distributed Consistency

Future research SHOULD investigate consistency models for organizations operating across:

* unreliable networks;
* partial failures;
* asynchronous execution;
* geographically distributed environments.

---

# 7. Organizational Learning

Research SHOULD determine:

* when Experience becomes Knowledge;
* how Knowledge is validated;
* how obsolete Knowledge is detected;
* how conflicting Knowledge is resolved;
* how Knowledge confidence evolves.

---

# 8. Trust

Future research SHOULD formalize Trust as a function of:

```text
Trust =
f(
    Evidence,
    Provenance,
    Validation,
    History,
    Policy
)
```

Trust SHALL remain evidence-based.

---

# 9. Organizational Resilience

Research SHOULD measure the ability of a Cognitive Organization to preserve:

* identity;
* Goals;
* Memory;
* OCS;
* governance;
* Capabilities;

during execution failures.

---

# 10. Emergent Organizational Behavior

Research SHOULD investigate whether organizational intelligence emerges from interactions between:

* Roles;
* Capabilities;
* Knowledge;
* Policies;
* Evidence;
* Decisions.

---

# 11. Multi-Organization Cognition

Future versions MAY extend TDOC to interactions between independent Cognitive Organizations.

Conceptually:

```text
Organization A
      ↕
Organization B
      ↕
Organization C
```

Each organization SHALL preserve its own:

* identity;
* Goals;
* governance;
* Memory;
* authoritative OCS.

---

# 12. Formal Verification

Future VIAL tooling SHOULD investigate automated verification of:

* invariants;
* Policies;
* state transitions;
* provenance;
* semantic consistency.

---

# 13. Benchmark Science

Future benchmark specifications SHOULD establish reproducible measurements for:

* token consumption;
* latency;
* Cognitive Cost;
* Decision Quality;
* state consistency;
* recovery;
* scalability;
* organizational learning.

---

# 14. Central Research Hypothesis

The primary research hypothesis of VIAL is:

> Shared organizational cognition can reduce redundant reasoning and communication while maintaining or improving decision quality.

This hypothesis SHALL be experimentally evaluated.

---

# 15. Long-Term Research

The TDOC MAY eventually expand into a broader theory covering:

* Distributed Cognition;
* Organizational Intelligence;
* Collective Decision Systems;
* Autonomous Organizations;
* Machine Organizational Learning;
* Cognitive Governance;
* Organizational Self-Optimization.

---

# 16. Research Governance

Research results SHALL NOT automatically become normative VIAL architecture.

A research result becomes normative only after passing through the VIAL governance process.

The expected progression is:

```text
Hypothesis
   ↓
Research
   ↓
Experiment
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Review
   ↓
ADR / FCP / RFC
   ↓
Normative Specification
```

---

# 17. Success Criterion

The long-term success of the TDOC is not merely the creation of another multi-agent framework.

Its success criterion is the establishment of a generalizable theory and architecture capable of producing organizations that are:

* efficient;
* scalable;
* auditable;
* resilient;
* economically viable;
* cognitively coherent.
