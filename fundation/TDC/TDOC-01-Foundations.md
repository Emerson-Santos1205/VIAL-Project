# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the foundational entities and principles upon which the Theory of Distributed Organizational Cognition (TDOC) is built.

All subsequent chapters derive from the concepts defined herein.

---

# 2. Fundamental Premise

The primary subject of the TDOC is not the individual intelligent agent.

The primary subject is the **Cognitive Organization**.

Agents are execution resources.

Organizations are cognitive systems.

---

# 3. Cognitive Organization

A Cognitive Organization is an autonomous organizational system created to achieve one or more goals through coordinated cognition.

A Cognitive Organization is defined by:

- Goals
- Organizational Cognition
- Organizational Cognitive State
- Roles
- Capabilities
- Policies
- Decisions
- Evidence
- Organizational Memory

---

# 4. Organizational Goal

Every Cognitive Organization SHALL exist for one or more explicit Goals.

Goals define purpose.

Goals justify organizational existence.

Without Goals, no Cognitive Organization exists.

---

# 5. Organizational Cognition

Organizational Cognition represents the collective understanding accumulated by the organization.

It is composed of:

- Knowledge
- Intent
- Context
- Evidence
- State

Organizational Cognition evolves continuously.

---

# 6. Organizational Cognitive State (OCS)

Every Cognitive Organization SHALL maintain exactly one Organizational Cognitive State.

The OCS represents the authoritative cognitive state of the organization at a specific moment.

The OCS is:

- unique;
- shared;
- synchronized;
- auditable;
- versioned.

All organizational decisions affect the OCS.

---

# 7. Organizational Memory

Organizational Memory is the persistent repository of validated organizational knowledge.

Organizational Memory survives:

- agent replacement;
- runtime replacement;
- infrastructure migration;
- organizational restructuring.

Organizational Memory SHALL preserve organizational continuity.

---

# 8. Roles

Roles define organizational responsibilities.

Roles are independent of implementation.

Multiple agents MAY perform the same Role.

One agent MAY perform multiple Roles.

---

# 9. Capabilities

Capabilities represent reusable organizational abilities.

Capabilities are associated with Roles.

Capabilities are independent of specific agents.

Capabilities MAY evolve over time.

---

# 10. Policies

Policies define organizational constraints.

Policies regulate decisions.

Policies SHALL NOT directly execute actions.

Policies define acceptable organizational behavior.

---

# 11. Decisions

A Decision is a validated organizational action that modifies the Organizational Cognitive State.

Every Decision SHALL:

- reference one or more Goals;
- comply with Policies;
- be supported by Evidence;
- be auditable.

---

# 12. Evidence

Evidence is observable information supporting organizational decisions.

Evidence contributes to organizational confidence.

Evidence SHALL be traceable.

Evidence SHALL remain associated with the Decisions it supports.

---

# 13. Organizational Evolution

Organizations continuously evolve through validated Decisions.

Organizational evolution is represented as successive transitions of the Organizational Cognitive State.

No organization is considered static.

---

# 14. Organizational Continuity

Organizations SHALL preserve continuity despite:

- agent replacement;
- infrastructure changes;
- software updates;
- execution failures.

Continuity is achieved through preservation of the Organizational Cognitive State and Organizational Memory.

---

# 15. Foundational Relationships

The foundational relationship is defined as:

Goal

↓

Organization

↓

Roles

↓

Capabilities

↓

Decisions

↓

Organizational Cognitive State

↓

Organizational Cognition

↓

Organizational Memory

This relationship forms the minimum conceptual model of the TDOC.

---

# 16. Foundation Principles

The TDOC is based on the following principles:

1. Organizations exist to achieve Goals.
2. Cognition belongs to Organizations.
3. Agents execute organizational responsibilities.
4. Decisions transform Organizational Cognitive State.
5. Organizational Memory preserves knowledge.
6. Evidence validates decisions.
7. Policies constrain organizational behavior.
8. Organizational evolution is continuous.

These principles constitute the conceptual foundation upon which the remaining chapters of the TDOC are constructed.
